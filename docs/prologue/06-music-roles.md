# PROLOGUE — MUSIC ROLES

**No track is chosen here.** Music is a major editorial component and the
choice is the user's. This file defines what each part needs the music to *do*,
so that when tracks are auditioned there is something to audition them against.

---

## THE STRUCTURAL QUESTION — one score or three?

The brief asks not to assume three songs. Having laid the three beat boards out,
the recommendation is:

> **One evolving score across all three parts, with two hard breaks.**

### Why one score

The prologue's whole argument is *continuity*: speed → understanding → scale →
history. Three separate tracks would announce three separate videos and undo
that argument at exactly the two moments it most needs to hold — the handoff
from "this is an arena" into Clan Arena, and from "again, again, again" into the
archive. A single evolving score makes those handoffs feel like consequences
rather than chapter cards.

### Why two hard breaks anyway

Part 1's beat 9 is **1.2 seconds of total silence** after seven events in 1.5 s.
That silence is load-bearing — it is what makes the chaos legible instead of
noisy. And Part 3's beat 10 is a hard cut to black before THE MACHINE.

Both are **score events, not absences**: the music stops dead and restarts
changed. A single track that can be cut to silence twice and come back
transformed is the requirement.

### The cost of getting this wrong

If a track cannot survive being stopped dead at 7.5 s and restarted at 8.7 s,
it is the wrong track — no matter how good the first 7 seconds are. **Audition
that cut first, before anything else.**

---

## PART 1 — IMMEDIACY

| Need | Why |
|---|---|
| **Cuttable at 0.1 s resolution** | The chaos burst runs 0.2 s units. A track with long attack transients smears them. |
| **Strong short accents, not sustained pads** | Every accent must be able to land on a frag. |
| **Survives a dead stop and a cold restart** | Beat 9. Non-negotiable. |
| **No vocal hook in the first 12 s** | Words compete with `QUAKE.` and `FAST ARENA FPS`. |
| **Escalating density, not escalating volume** | P1-G: level is fixed. The *arrangement* escalates, never the gain. |

**Tempo guidance:** the cut grid wants a tempo where 0.2 s is a clean
subdivision — roughly **150 BPM** (0.2 s = a 16th at 150) or **120 BPM**
(0.25 s = a 16th). Pick the grid first, then cut to it.

---

## PART 2 — CLARITY

| Need | Why |
|---|---|
| **Legible pulse, low density** | The audience is being taught. Anything busy competes with the alive counter. |
| **Room for the real countdown announcer** | 5-3-2-1-FIGHT is diegetic and must sit on top. Leave the midrange empty at beats 4–5. |
| **A genuine lift at FIGHT** | The countdown builds tension the game itself wrote; the score should pay it. |
| **Can drop to almost nothing at the freeze** | Beat 10 (the 2v2 freeze) needs space. |
| **Resolution at ROUND WON** | Beat 12 must feel like an ending, because beat 13 immediately takes it away with the reset. |

The reset (beats 13–15) is where Part 2 hands over: the same figure repeating,
tightening, multiplying. **The score should loop and compress there** — that is
the transition into Part 3, and it should be musical rather than a cut.

---

## PART 3 — SCALE

| Need | Why |
|---|---|
| **Low, wide, patient opening** | The archive is asleep. Beats 1–8 are a slow reveal. |
| **Accumulation rather than melody** | Layers arriving as counters climb. The music does what the visuals do. |
| **A full stop at beat 10** | Hard cut to black before THE MACHINE. Second hard break. |
| **Mechanical, precise texture for THE MACHINE** | Beats 11–12 are queries resolving. Sequenced, gridded, inhuman. |
| **Human warmth for THE HUMAN** | Beats 13–16 are the argument. The texture must change audibly when a person enters. |
| **Unresolved ending** | Beat 20 hands off to the movie. It must want to continue. |

**The machine/human contrast is the score's most important job in Part 3.** If
those two passages sound the same, the film's thesis — *the machine organises,
the human curates* — is stated in text and contradicted in sound.

---

## RULES INHERITED FROM THE PROJECT

- **P1-G v6 applies in full.** One fixed level throughout. **No sidechain
  ducking, no level-following of any kind.** Sync comes from matching the
  action to the music, never from modulating music gain to the action. The only
  permitted level moves are a fade-in at the very start and a fade-out at the
  very end — plus the two scored silences above, which are *arrangement*, not
  gain automation.
- **Never time-stretch or resample for pitch.** The picture bends to the beat
  grid; the music is untouched.
- **Game audio is diegetic and stays.** The footstep at beat 0, the countdown
  announcer, weapon sounds. The prologue is partly *about* how Quake sounds.

---

## WHAT TO AUDITION FIRST

Before evaluating any track musically, test it against these three cuts:

1. **The dead stop.** Part 1 beat 7.5 s → silence → 8.7 s restart. Does it
   survive?
2. **The countdown.** Can the announcer sit on top at Part 2 beats 4–5 without
   fighting it?
3. **The machine/human turn.** Part 3 beat 13. Can this track change character
   there without changing track?

A track that fails any of the three is wrong for the prologue regardless of how
it sounds in isolation.
